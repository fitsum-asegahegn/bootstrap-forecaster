Bootstrap Small‑Sample Forecaster

Paste 12–24 months of revenue, sales, or cash-flow figures and get back a full probability distribution for what happens next — median, worst case, and the exact odds of crossing a number you care about. Built for businesses too small for ARIMA and too data-scarce for confident guessing.

Second link in the chain: the same block-bootstrap and Monte Carlo machinery that stress-tested bank portfolio risk in the author's thesis, and priced forex-spread exposure for importers in the sibling tool, applied here directly to a single business's own ledger — no minimum data history required. Methodology: A Bootstrap-Based Forecasting Framework for Small Business Samples (Asegahegn, 2026).
⚠ Demo methodology — validated on synthetic SME-pattern data (Section 4 of the paper), not yet backtested against real Ethiopian ledger series

    How this works

    The series is split into a systematic part and a residual part. If a linear trend is statistically detectable, it's fit by ordinary least squares; otherwise the systematic part is just the sample average. What's left over — the residuals — is resampled using a circular block bootstrap, with a block length of ⌊n/3⌋, so that short-run dependence between consecutive months is preserved instead of being shuffled away.

    For each of 3,000 bootstrap replicates, a new residual sequence is drawn, the trend is re-estimated on the reconstructed series (capturing estimation uncertainty, not just noise), extrapolated forward to the requested horizon, and combined with an independently resampled future residual. The resulting 3,000 future paths form the empirical predictive distribution shown in the fan chart and histogram above.

    Intervals here are computed with the direct percentile method rather than the bias-corrected-and-accelerated (BCa) adjustment specified in the paper — a reasonable proxy for small, roughly symmetric residual sets, but one that can understate skew. A future version may add the BCa bias/acceleration correction client-side.
